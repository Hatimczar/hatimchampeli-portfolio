Apple quietly reshaped enterprise IT this year, and if your organization runs on Apple Business Manager, JAMF, or both, it's worth catching up on what changed and what it means from a Dubai or Abu Dhabi office.

## What launched

In April, Apple discontinued three separate services, Apple Business Manager, Apple Business Essentials, and Apple Business Connect, and folded them into one platform: **Apple Business**.

The headline feature is built-in MDM at no extra cost. Apple Business lets you:

- Configure device settings, security policies, and available apps from one place
- Use **Blueprints** to pre-configure devices for true zero-touch deployment
- Assign **Managed Apple Accounts**, which keep work and personal data cryptographically separated on the same device
- Auto-provision new hires through existing Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra ID directories
- Run an employee app for installing work apps, requesting IT support, and browsing the company directory

It's free, no monthly device management fee, with optional paid add-ons for extra iCloud storage and AppleCare+. It's live in over 200 countries and regions, which includes the UAE from day one.

## Why Jamf is moving fast

Apple building MDM directly into a free platform is a direct shot at the third-party MDM market that Jamf, Mosyle, and others have built their businesses on. Jamf isn't standing still: the company brought in **Beth Tschida as its new CEO**, succeeding John Strosahl, and closed its **biggest acquisition to date**, a $400 million deal for Wandera, a mobile security and network intelligence company, to push further into the compliance and security territory that a free, lighter-touch Apple tool isn't built to cover.

## The UAE decision

For a small team in Dubai running twenty MacBooks and a handful of iPads, Apple Business's free tier is a genuine option. Blueprints alone can replace a chunk of what a smaller org was paying JAMF for.

For anything larger, or anything touching regulated data, the calculus doesn't change as much as the headlines suggest. DIFC and ADGM entities, government-adjacent organizations, and any UAE business managing devices under a compliance framework will still lean on JAMF or Mosyle for the depth Apple Business doesn't attempt to replicate: granular compliance reporting, deep third-party integrations, and the kind of audit trail regulators actually ask for.

The honest read for most of my UAE clients: start evaluating Apple Business now if you're a smaller operation, but don't rip out JAMF this quarter if you're managing a fleet that answers to a regulator. Apple Business is one year old. Give it a cycle before you trust it with anything you can't afford to get wrong.
